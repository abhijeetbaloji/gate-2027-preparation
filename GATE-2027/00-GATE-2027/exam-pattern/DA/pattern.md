## Official record for DA

DA is one of the 30 GATE 2027 test papers. The examination is in English.

| Particular | Official detail for DA |
| --- | --- |
| Mode | Computer Based Test (CBT) |
| Language | English |
| Duration | 3 hours. Brochure section 7.1: all test papers are of three hours duration (a maximum of four hours for candidates availing compensatory time). After the duration has elapsed, the examination terminates automatically. |
| Number of questions | 10 (General Aptitude) + 55 (subject) = 65 questions |
| Total marks | 100 |
| General Aptitude (GA) | 15 marks. Common to all papers. Brochure section 7.3.1: the GA section includes 5 questions carrying 1 mark each and 5 questions carrying 2 marks each. |
| Subject questions | 85 marks |
| Engineering Mathematics as a separate marks line | Not used for DA. DA is in the group for which the published distribution is General Aptitude 15 marks and subject questions 85 marks. |
| Mark value of a question | 1 mark or 2 marks |
| Total time shown on the pattern table | 180 minutes |

The Question Paper Pattern page and Brochure Table 8 place DA in this row:

| Paper codes | General Aptitude | Subject common section | Subject section(s) | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CY, DA, EY, MA, PH, ST | 15 | 85 | — | 100 |

Brochure section 7.3 states: in test papers AR, CY, DA, EY, GE, GG, MA, PH, RA, ST, XH and XL, the General Aptitude section carries 15 marks and the remaining 85 marks are devoted to the subject component.

The 13-mark Engineering Mathematics allocation is published for a different group: AE, AG, BM, BT, CE, CH, CS, EC, EE, ES, IN, ME, MN, MT, NM, PE, and PI. DA is not in that group.

The Test Papers & Syllabus page states that each GATE 2027 paper is for a total of 100 marks, General Aptitude is common for all papers (15 marks), and the rest of the paper covers the respective test paper syllabus (85 marks).

## Marking rules that apply to DA

From the Question Paper Pattern page:

- For a wrong answer chosen in an MCQ, there will be negative marking.
- For a 1-mark MCQ, 1/3 mark will be deducted for a wrong answer.
- For a 2-mark MCQ, 2/3 mark will be deducted for a wrong answer.
- There is no negative marking for wrong answer(s) to MSQ or NAT questions.
- There is no partial marking in MSQ.

From Information Brochure section 4, the negative-marking paragraph is:

- For a wrong answer to an MCQ, there will be negative marking.
- For a 1-mark MCQ, 1/3 will be deducted for a wrong answer. For a 2-mark MCQ, 2/3 will be deducted for a wrong answer.
- There is no negative marking for wrong answers to MSQ and NAT questions.
- There is no partial marking for any question.

Brochure section 7.2.1 states the MCQ deduction as “1/3 of a mark” and “2/3 of a mark”, and adds:

- MSQ: there is no negative marking for a wrong answer. There is no partial mark for choosing partially correct combinations of choices or any single wrong choice.
- NAT: there is no negative marking for a wrong answer. There is no partial marking in NAT questions.

Question types, NAT entry rules, and the descriptions of recall, comprehension, application, and analysis and synthesis are in `../general-question-pattern.md`.

## What the official DA pattern does not state

The Question Paper Pattern page and Brochure sections 7.3 and Table 8 do not publish, for DA, how many of the 55 subject questions carry 1 mark and how many carry 2 marks. That split is not recorded here.

DA does not have the Part A / Part B choice used for AR, GE, GG, and RA, and it does not have the optional sections used for XE, XH, and XL.

## Other official notes that apply to a DA candidate

- A candidate appears for one or two papers. The second paper must be an allowed combination. DA combinations are in `../../important-information/two-paper-combination.md`.
- Some test papers may be held in multiple sessions. A candidate appears in only one session of a paper, on the date and time on the admit card. Brochure highlights: the examination is held in two sessions on the examination dates, forenoon and afternoon.
- Only the on-screen virtual calculator provided during the examination is to be used.
- Compensatory time is governed by O.M. F. No. P-13013/75/2023-Policy-DD-III dated 1 August 2025. The pattern page states one hour for specified categories. Brochure section 4 states 20 minutes per hour. Both statements are quoted in `../general-question-pattern.md`.

## Official description

Homepage:

- GATE is a national-level examination that evaluates candidates’ understanding of undergraduate-level subjects in Engineering, Technology, Science, Commerce, Arts, Architecture, and Humanities.
- It is conducted jointly by IISc and the IITs on behalf of the National Coordination Board (NCB), Department of Higher Education, Ministry of Education (MoE), Government of India.
- Qualified candidates can use GATE scores for admission to postgraduate and doctoral programs, with possible financial assistance, in MoE-supported institutions. Several public sector undertakings also use GATE scores for recruitment.
- The homepage states “30 Test Papers” and “3 years validity”.

Opportunities page:

> Graduate Aptitude Test in Engineering (GATE) is a prestigious national-level examination that assesses candidates for comprehensive understanding in various undergraduate-level subjects in Engineering/Technology/Architecture/Science/Commerce/Arts/Humanities for admission to various Master’s and Doctoral Programs and Recruitment by some Public Sector Undertakings.

Organizing institute for GATE 2027: IIT Madras. Official website stated in the brochure: https://gate2027.iitm.ac.in.

## Admission

A valid GATE score can be used for seeking admission to:

- Master’s programs and direct doctoral programs in Engineering / Technology / Architecture / Science / Humanities.
- Doctoral programs in relevant branches of Engineering / Technology / Architecture / Science / Commerce / Arts / Humanities, in institutions supported by the MoE and other Government agencies.

The FAQ states that admission to any institute is solely subject to that institute’s own eligibility and selection criteria, and that admission is not under the purview of the GATE 2027 organizing institute.

## Financial assistance

A qualifying GATE score can be used for seeking financial assistance towards the same two classes of programs listed under admission.

Published amounts on the Opportunities page:

- Financial assistance given to M.Tech. students is ₹12,400 per month and is generally paid for 22 months. The page says to refer to “PG Scholarship (GATE/GPAT)” for details. The link on that page points at `static/doc/references/aicte-GUIDELINES FOR PG scholarship.pdf` on https://gate2027.iitm.ac.in/.
- Financial assistance given for Ph.D. programs (direct Ph.D. after B.E. / B.Tech. / M.Sc. equivalent with a valid GATE score) is ₹37,000 per month for the first two years and ₹42,000 per month from the third to the fifth year. The page cites Ministry of Finance, Department of Expenditure Circular No. 33(14)PFC-II/2018 dated 21 June 2023. The link on that page points at `static/doc/references/OM Fellowship June 2023.pdf` on https://gate2027.iitm.ac.in/.

## Score, qualifying marks, and validity

These statements are from the Information Brochure and the FAQ, not from the Opportunities page.

Brochure section 8.2:

- GATE 2027 results will be announced on the GATE 2027 website.
- Scorecards will be available for download by qualified candidates.
- A fee of ₹500 per test paper per candidate is levied for obtaining the scorecard after the download period announced on the website.
- From 1 January 2028 onward, scorecards will not be issued for GATE 2027 qualified candidates.
- The GATE 2027 scorecard is valid for three years from the date of announcement of the results. No information on the GATE 2027 score will be available after this period.
- There is no provision for a hard copy of the GATE 2027 scorecard.

FAQ:

- The score card is valid for three years. The validity is also mentioned on the score card.
- The score card is issued only to candidates who have secured marks greater than or equal to the qualifying marks mentioned for the SC/ST/PwD category of that paper.
- During the free download window at the end of March, there is no fee. After that period, the score card can be downloaded from GOAPS by paying ₹500 up to 31 December 2027.
- Marks shown on the GOAPS portal are for information only. An electronic or paper copy of that display is not valid.
- Net total marks for some candidates may be negative.
- The formula that translates marks into a GATE score can produce a negative GATE score even when marks are positive. The FAQ points to the Information Brochure for the equation.
- A data-correction window for candidate details will be open during October 2026. After a score card is issued, the FAQ says it is not possible to change the data on issued score cards, because that data is already shared with admitting institutes, COAP, and PSUs.

Brochure section 8.1, qualifying marks for the General category in each subject:

> Qualifying marks for GENERAL category = max(25, min(40, μ + σ))

where μ is the mean and σ is the standard deviation of the marks of all candidates who appeared for the test paper.

- OBC-NCL / EWS qualifying marks = (9/10) × qualifying marks for the General category.
- SC / ST / PwD qualifying marks = (2/3) × qualifying marks for the General category.

The FAQ states the EWS qualifying mark as 90% of the General qualifying mark. That matches the brochure ratio of 9/10. The FAQ also states that the EWS application fee is the same as the General fee.

The brochure gives the GATE score formula as:

> GATE Score = Sq + (St − Sq) × (M − Mq) / (M̄t − Mq)

where M is the marks obtained (actual marks for a single-session paper and normalized marks for a multi-session paper); Mq is the qualifying marks for the General category candidate in the paper; M̄t is the mean of the marks of the top 0.1% or top 10, whichever is larger, of the candidates who appeared in the paper (all sessions, for a multi-session paper); Sq = 350; and St = 900.

For multi-session papers, the brochure prints a normalization formula. In words, the normalized marks of the j-th candidate in the i-th session are:

> M̂ij = ((M̄tg − Mqg) / (M̄ti − Miq)) × (Mij − Miq) + Mqg

where Mij is the actual marks of that candidate in that session; M̄tg is the average marks of the top 0.1% of candidates considering all sessions; Mqg is the sum of the mean and standard deviation of marks of the candidates in the paper considering all sessions; M̄ti is the average marks of the top 0.1% of candidates in the i-th session; and Miq is the sum of the mean and standard deviation of the i-th session.

The brochure states that this normalization assumes that the distribution of abilities of candidates is the same across sessions.

> GATE scores may be shared with institutions supported by the MoE, Public Sector Undertakings, and other eminent institutions for the purpose of admission and recruitment.

- Qualifying in GATE does not guarantee admission, scholarship, or employment. Admission to any institute is solely subject to that institute’s own eligibility and selection criteria.
- GATE qualification does not assure a PSU job. No responsibility is assumed by the organizing institute for admission, scholarship, or recruitment outcomes.

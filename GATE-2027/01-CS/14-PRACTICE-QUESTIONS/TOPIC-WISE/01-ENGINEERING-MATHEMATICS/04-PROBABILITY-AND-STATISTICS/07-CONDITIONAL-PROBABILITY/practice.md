## Detailed Solutions

### Q1

\[
P(A\mid B)=\frac{P(A\cap B)}{P(B)}=\frac{3/25}{2/5}=\frac{3}{25}\cdot\frac{5}{2}=\frac{3}{10}.
\]

Option (B) is the joint probability, left undivided. Option (C) is the product \(P(A\cap B)P(B)\). Option (D) is \(P(B)\) itself. Conditioning on \(B\) divides the joint probability by \(P(B)\). Answer: **A**.

### Q2

The multiplication rule gives

\[
P(A\cap B)=P(A)\,P(B\mid A)=\frac{1}{4}\cdot\frac{3}{5}=\frac{3}{20}.
\]

Then \(100\cdot(3/20)=15\). Adding the given probabilities produces \(1/4+3/5=17/20\), and \(100\) times that sum is \(85\), which is not a joint probability. Answer: **15**.

### Q3

The even outcomes are \(\{2,4,6\}\), and exactly one of them is \(6\). Each face of a fair die is equally likely, so

\[
P(\{6\}\mid\text{even})=\frac{1}{3}.
\]

Option (A) is the unconditional probability of rolling \(6\). Option (C) would be correct for a comparison such as “greater than \(3\)” only under a different conditioning event. Option (D) is \(P(\{4,6\}\mid\text{even})\), the conditional probability of a result above \(3\). Answer: **B**.

### Q4

(A) is the definition of conditional probability, which requires \(P(B)>0\).

(B) is false. The two conditionals divide the same joint probability by different marginals, so they agree only in special cases.

(C) is an equivalent form of independence when \(P(B)>0\): knowing \(B\) does not change the probability of \(A\).

(D) is false. Division by \(P(B)\) is undefined when \(P(B)=0\).

(E) is the multiplication rule, rearranged from the definition of \(P(B\mid A)\).

Answer: **A, C**.

### Q5

After a red ball is removed, \(4\) red balls remain among \(11\) balls. Thus

\[
P(\text{second red}\mid\text{first red})=\frac{4}{11}.
\]

Option (A) is the probability that the first ball is red. Option (C) would be appropriate only if five red balls remained among eleven, which does not happen after a red draw. Option (D) is \(4/12\), which reduces the red count but not the total. Without replacement, both counts change. Answer: **B**.

### Q6

Independence means the joint probability is the product:

\[
P(A\cap B)=\frac{2}{5}\cdot\frac{1}{4}=\frac{1}{10}.
\]

Here \(p=1\), \(q=10\), and \(p+q=11\). The fraction \(2/20\) is the same value but is not in lowest terms; its numerator and denominator would add to \(22\). Adding the probabilities instead gives \(2/5+1/4=13/20\), and \(13+20=33\). Answer: **11**.

### Q7

The morning and afternoon cases partition the requests. The law of total probability gives

\[
P(\text{fault})=\frac{7}{10}\cdot\frac{1}{50}+\frac{3}{10}\cdot\frac{1}{20}=\frac{7}{500}+\frac{3}{200}=\frac{14}{1000}+\frac{15}{1000}=\frac{29}{1000}.
\]

Option (B) is the unweighted average of the two fault rates, \((1/50+1/20)/2=7/200\). That average would be correct only if the two periods were equally likely. Options (C) and (D) are the separate conditional fault rates, not the overall probability. Answer: **A**.

### Q8

The product of the marginals is

\[
P(A)P(B)=\frac{3}{10}\cdot\frac{1}{2}=\frac{3}{20}=P(A\cap B),
\]

so \(A\) and \(B\) are independent and (A) is true. The intersection is not empty of probability, so they are not mutually exclusive and (B) is false.

\[
P(A\mid B)=\frac{3/20}{1/2}=\frac{3}{10},\qquad P(B\mid A)=\frac{3/20}{3/10}=\frac{1}{2}.
\]

Thus (C) is true and (D) is false: (D) swaps the two conditionals. Finally,

\[
P(A\cup B)=\frac{3}{10}+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3}{20}=\frac{6}{20}+\frac{10}{20}-\frac{3}{20}=\frac{13}{20},
\]

so (E) is true. Answer: **A, C**.

### Q9

The chain rule multiplies along the conditions in order:

\[
P(A\cap B\cap C)=P(A)\,P(B\mid A)\,P(C\mid A\cap B)=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{1}{4}=\frac{1}{24}.
\]

Thus \(p+q=1+24=25\). Stopping after two factors gives either \(1/6\) or \(1/12\), whose numerator-denominator sums are \(7\) and \(13\). Answer: **25**.

### Q10

Eight equally likely outcomes exist. Exactly one of them, TTT, has no head, so seven outcomes remain after conditioning on at least one head. Only HHH has three heads. Therefore the conditional probability is \(1/7\).

Option (A) is the unconditional probability of HHH. Option (B) would be the answer for a single coin that is known to be heads, or for an incorrect reduction to two remaining outcomes. Option (D) is \(2/8\). Conditioning removes TTT from the sample space and does not leave the original eight outcomes. Answer: **C**.

### Q11

\[
P(\text{both white})=\frac{4}{10}\cdot\frac{3}{9}=\frac{12}{90}=\frac{2}{15}.
\]

The same value is \(\binom{4}{2}/\binom{10}{2}=(6)/(45)=2/15\). Thus \(p+q=2+15=17\). Treating the draws as independent gives \((4/10)^2=4/25\), and \(4+25=29\). The conditional probability that the second sock is white given that the first is white is \(3/9=1/3\), and \(1+3=4\); that is one factor, not the probability of both. Answer: **17**.

### Q12

The routes partition the possibilities:

\[
\begin{align*}
P(\text{late})
&=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{5}+\frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{6}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\\
&=\frac{1}{10}+\frac{1}{12}+\frac{1}{12}
=\frac{1}{10}+\frac{1}{6}
=\frac{3}{30}+\frac{5}{30}
=\frac{8}{30}
=\frac{4}{15}.
\end{align*}
\]

Option (B) is the lateness probability on \(R_1\). Option (C) is the lateness probability on \(R_2\). Option (D) is the unweighted average of the three conditional lateness probabilities:

\[
\frac{1/5+1/4+1/2}{3}=\frac{19/20}{3}=\frac{19}{60}.
\]

That average treats the routes as equally likely. The correct combination weights each conditional probability by the route probability. Answer: **A**.

### Q13

\[
P(A\cap B)=P(B)\,P(A\mid B)=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{2}{5}=\frac{1}{5},
\]

so (A) is true. Then

\[
P(B\mid A)=\frac{1/5}{3/5}=\frac{1}{3},
\]

so (B) is true. Independence would require \(P(A)P(B)=(3/5)(1/2)=3/10\), but the joint probability is \(1/5\neq 3/10\), so (C) is false. Also

\[
P(A\cap B^c)=P(A)-P(A\cap B)=\frac{3}{5}-\frac{1}{5}=\frac{2}{5},
\]

and \(P(B^c)=1/2\), so \(P(A\mid B^c)=(2/5)/(1/2)=4/5\). Thus (D) is true. Finally \(2/5\neq 1/3\), so (E) is false. Answer: **A, B, D**.

### Q14

The multiplication rule and the partition \(\{A,A^c\}\) give

\[
P(B)=P(B\mid A)P(A)+P(B\mid A^c)P(A^c)
=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{3}{5}+\frac{1}{4}\cdot\frac{2}{5}
=\frac{3}{10}+\frac{1}{10}
=\frac{2}{5}.
\]

The joint probability \(P(A\cap B)\) is \(3/10\), so

\[
P(A\mid B)=\frac{3/10}{2/5}=\frac{3}{4}.
\]

Option (A) is \(P(B\mid A)\), the reverse conditional. Option (C) is \(P(B)\). Option (D) is \(P(A)\). The question asks for the probability of \(A\) given \(B\), not the probability of \(B\) given \(A\). Answer: **B**.

### Q15

Mutually exclusive events satisfy \(P(A\cap B)=0\). Since \(P(B)=2/5>0\),

\[
P(A\mid B)=\frac{0}{2/5}=0.
\]

Option (B) is \(P(A)\), which would be \(P(A\mid B)\) if \(A\) and \(B\) were independent. They cannot be independent here unless one of them has probability \(0\), because independence would force \(P(A\cap B)=P(A)P(B)=2/25\neq 0\). Option (D) is that independence product. Option (C) is \(P(B)\). Mutual exclusion and independence are different relations. Answer: **A**.

### Q16

There are \(36\) equally likely outcomes. The outcomes with at least one \(6\) number \(6+6-1=11\). Exactly one of those outcomes is \((6,6)\). The conditional probability is \(1/11\), so \(p+q=12\).

The unconditional probability \(1/36\) gives \(1+36=37\). The probability that a single named die shows \(6\) is \(1/6\), and \(1+6=7\). Conditioning on “at least one \(6\)” leaves \(11\) outcomes, not \(36\) and not \(6\). Answer: **12**.

### Q17

The number of ways to draw two chips from nine is \(\binom{9}{2}=36\). The draws with no red chip are the draws from the five non-red chips, \(\binom{5}{2}=10\). So \(26\) draws have at least one red chip. The draws with two red chips number \(\binom{4}{2}=6\). Therefore the draws with exactly one red chip number \(26-6=20\), and

\[
P(\text{exactly one red}\mid\text{at least one red})=\frac{20}{26}=\frac{10}{13}.
\]

Thus \(p+q=10+13=23\). The unconditional probability of exactly one red chip is \(20/36=5/9\), and \(5+9=14\). That calculation forgets to restrict the sample space to the \(26\) draws that satisfy the condition. Answer: **23**.

### Q18

Independence gives \(P(A\cap B)=(1/2)(1/3)=1/6\). Then

\[
P(A\cup B)=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{3}-\frac{1}{6}=\frac{3}{6}+\frac{2}{6}-\frac{1}{6}=\frac{4}{6}=\frac{2}{3}.
\]

The event \(A\) is contained in \(A\cup B\), so \(P(A\cap(A\cup B))=P(A)=1/2\). Therefore

\[
P(A\mid A\cup B)=\frac{1/2}{2/3}=\frac{3}{4}.
\]

Option (B) is \(P(A)\), which ignores the conditioning. Option (C) is \(P(A\cup B)\) itself. Option (D) is \(P(A)+P(B)\), the union formula without subtracting the intersection. Answer: **A**.

# Propositional and First-Order Logic — Mistake Log

### Common Mistakes

**General/common trap:** Treating P→Q as "P causes Q" — it is false only at (T,F).

**General/common trap:** Confusing converse (Q→P) with contrapositive (¬Q→¬P) — only contrapositive is equivalent.

**General/common trap:** Writing ¬(P→Q) as ¬P∨¬Q instead of **P∧¬Q**.

**General/common trap:** "All A are B" as ∀x(A(x)∧B(x)) instead of ∀x(A(x)→B(x)).

**General/common trap:** Assuming ∀x∃y and ∃y∀x are equivalent — order of quantifiers matters.

**General/common trap:** Confusing valid argument with tautology — wrap premises in conjunction with → for tautology form.

**General/common trap:** Resolution on non-CNF formulas without converting first.

**General/common trap:** Forgetting operator precedence: ¬ > ∧ > ∨ > → > ↔.
